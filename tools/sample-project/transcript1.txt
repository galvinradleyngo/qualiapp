Interviewer: Thanks for sitting down with me. To start, can you tell me a bit about how your first semester has been going?

Ana: Honestly, it's been a lot harder than I expected. In high school my parents kept track of my schedule for me, but here nobody reminds you about anything. The first few weeks I missed two quizzes because I just didn't check the syllabus closely enough.

Interviewer: What did you do once you realized that was happening?

Ana: I started writing everything down in a planner, like literally every deadline the moment a professor mentions it. It sounds simple, but it took me almost a month to build that habit. Before that I was constantly anxious, like I was forgetting something important even when I wasn't.

Interviewer: You mentioned anxiety — can you say more about that?

Ana: Yeah, especially at night. I'd lie awake going through my mental list of assignments. I also just missed home a lot in the beginning. My mom used to check in on me every evening, and suddenly there was this silence where that used to be. I called her almost every day the first month just to hear a familiar voice.

Interviewer: Did that get easier over time?

Ana: It did, once I found people going through the same thing. My roommate was also a first-year, and we started studying together in the evenings. Just having someone to complain to about a hard professor made it feel less overwhelming. We also joined a study group for our math class, and honestly that group is the only reason I passed my first exam.

Interviewer: What about your professors — did you reach out to any of them?

Ana: Not at first. I was scared they'd think I was slow for asking basic questions. But one of my professors mentioned during office hours that half the class was struggling with the same topic, and that made me feel less alone. After that I started going to office hours regularly, and it genuinely changed how I felt about the class. I went from dreading it to actually looking forward to it.

Interviewer: Looking back at the whole semester, how would you describe the change in yourself?

Ana: I think I trust myself more now. At the start I felt like I needed someone else to tell me I was doing okay. Now I can look at my own planner and know I'm on top of things. I still get overwhelmed sometimes, especially during exam weeks, but I don't panic the way I used to. I know what steps to take now.
