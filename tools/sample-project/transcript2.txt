Interviewer: Can you walk me through what a typical week looked like for you early in the semester?

Marco: Chaotic, honestly. I was working a part-time job on weekends and trying to keep up with five classes. Nobody really tells you how much reading is expected in college compared to high school. I remember opening one syllabus and seeing three hundred pages assigned for a single week and just staring at it.

Interviewer: How did you cope with that workload?

Marco: Badly, at first. I pulled a few all-nighters trying to catch up, which just made me more tired and behind the next day. It wasn't sustainable. Eventually I talked to my older cousin who had already graduated, and she suggested I drop one of my elective units. That felt like giving up at the time, but it was honestly the best decision I made that semester.

Interviewer: What changed after you dropped that class?

Marco: I finally had breathing room. I could actually read the material instead of skimming it right before class. I also started going to the library instead of studying in my dorm, because my roommate was always playing games and it was hard to focus.

Interviewer: You mentioned feeling behind — did that affect how you felt about yourself as a student?

Marco: Definitely. There was a stretch where I felt like I didn't belong here, like everyone else had it figured out and I was the only one drowning. I didn't tell anyone that for a while because I was embarrassed. It wasn't until a classmate admitted she was struggling too that I realized a lot of us were faking confidence we didn't actually have.

Interviewer: Did talking about it with classmates help?

Marco: A lot. We ended up forming a small group chat just to vent and share tips, like which professors give practice exams or how to format citations. It became this informal support system. I also started messaging a professor directly when I was confused about an assignment instead of waiting for the next class, and she always responded quickly, which took a lot of pressure off.

Interviewer: If you could tell a new first-year student one thing, what would it be?

Marco: Ask for help earlier than feels comfortable. I wasted so much energy trying to look like I had it together instead of just admitting I was stuck. The moment I stopped doing that, everything got easier to manage.
