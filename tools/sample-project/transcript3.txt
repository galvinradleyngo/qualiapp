Interviewer: What was the biggest adjustment for you moving from high school to college?

Bea: Definitely the independence. In high school, teachers checked our notebooks and reminded us constantly about deadlines. Here, a professor just says the paper is due in three weeks and that's it — no reminders, no checking in. I had to learn to manage that on my own, and it took a while.

Interviewer: How did you handle that shift?

Bea: I struggled at first. I turned in one paper late because I completely lost track of the date, and the grade penalty was rough. After that I started using a digital calendar and setting reminders a few days before anything was due, not just the night before.

Interviewer: Beyond deadlines, was there anything emotionally difficult about the transition?

Bea: The homesickness hit harder than I expected. My family is close, and suddenly I was eating dinner alone most nights. I remember crying in my dorm room during the second week because everything felt unfamiliar — the food, the schedule, even just the silence of not having my siblings around.

Interviewer: How did you get through that period?

Bea: My dorm mates, honestly. One of them noticed I seemed down and invited me to eat with her group. Having people to sit with at meals made the biggest difference. We still eat together most days now. I also called my parents every Sunday, which became this ritual that helped me feel connected without being distracted from schoolwork during the week.

Interviewer: Did your confidence as a student change over the semester?

Bea: Yes, especially after my first big exam. I studied differently than I did in high school — I made summary sheets and quizzed myself instead of just re-reading notes — and it worked. Seeing that grade made me trust my own methods more. I stopped assuming professors expected perfection and started seeing mistakes as part of learning.

Interviewer: Anything you wish someone had told you before you started?

Bea: That it's okay to ask professors questions even if they seem busy. I avoided office hours the entire first month because I thought I'd be bothering them. When I finally went, my professor was patient and actually seemed happy I came. I wish I hadn't waited so long.
